The UAE rules

Where the UAE lands on an automotive business, specifically.

Global suites say “localised”. A UAE dealer, workshop or parts trader needs five specific things right.

  • The profit-margin scheme. VAT on eligible used-vehicle sales accounted for on the margin, per vehicle, with the FTA’s records kept; the system applies it, a person does not remember it.
  • Re-export. Vehicles and parts sold to Africa, the CIS and the Gulf carry customs status, destination and documents on the record, with the VAT treatment that follows.
  • GCC spec and imports. Spec, origin and customs cost flagged per VIN; landed cost right before the price is.
  • RTA and registration. Transfer status tracked on the vehicle record so a car is not sold twice or delivered unregistered.
  • WPS, EOSB, Corporate Tax, PINT-AE. Technicians and salespeople on WPS with SIF files, gratuity accruing, the 9% tracked, invoices leaving as e-invoices — the compliance ledger every UAE system carries, on the ERP page.

Before you sign

Six questions for any automotive ERP vendor in the UAE — including one four hours away.

Ask them in the demo, with your own vehicle and job card on the screen.

  1. 01

    Show me a used-vehicle sale under the profit-margin scheme.

    In the demo. If the VAT is calculated on the full price, the system does not know the UAE.

  2. 02

    How does a part issued to a job card hit the stock and the invoice at once?

    If it is two entries, the stock will be wrong by Thursday.

  3. 03

    Can the customer approve the estimate on WhatsApp?

    That is how UAE workshops actually get approvals. A portal nobody opens is not an approval.

  4. 04

    What happens to a supersession across three branches?

    The old number must find the new one everywhere, or the Sharjah warehouse sells what Al Quoz has just re-ordered.

  5. 05

    Where is the re-export paperwork?

    If it lives in a separate spreadsheet, it is not in the system.

  6. 06

    Will you say a garage-management app is enough?

    For a two-bay workshop with no parts business, it is. Anyone who never says so is selling.
