Buy, build, or not yet

The three answers no vendor page gives, in the order we give them.

We build custom systems. That is a bias, so here is the order we actually use on the first call.

Not yet

Keep the accounting package. Add discipline.

A stable business under about twenty users, one or two entities, standard processes: Zoho Books, QuickBooks or Tally kept clean, a stock count that is believed, a spreadsheet nobody edits without telling the owner. Software does not create discipline; buying it to impose discipline produces a system nobody uses.

Buy

A product, tested on the compliance flow with your data.

Odoo or ERPNext with a capable partner; Dynamics 365 Business Central or NetSuite for multi-entity groups; one of the UAE products ranking here if the sector fit is real. Watch one invoice go from despatch to PINT-AE to the ledger in the demo. Products that need a “localisation partner” for that are not UAE-ready.

Build

When the process is the reason you are still on spreadsheets.

Unusual pricing rules, multi-entity re-export, job work, a storefront that must read live stock — and two demos that ended in “we can configure that”. A system built around the process stops the workarounds. It costs more up front and less every year the per-user bill would have grown.

The e-invoice flow

From despatch to the FTA in one flow, or it is not e-invoicing.

PINT-AE is the UAE’s Peppol-based standard: the invoice leaves the system as structured data, passes through an accredited service provider for validation, and reaches the buyer and the Federal Tax Authority without anyone re-keying it. Phase 2 mandates from July 2026 per current Ministry of Finance guidance. Watch this happen in any demo before you sign; a system that produces a PDF and asks you to upload it has not done the work.

Before you sign

Six questions for any ERP vendor in the UAE — including one four hours away.

They apply to a product demo and to us word for word.

  1. 01

    Show one invoice go from despatch to PINT-AE to the ledger, with my data.

    In the demo, not in a slide. A product that needs a “localisation partner” for this is not UAE-ready.

  2. 02

    What happens on day fifteen of the fourteen-day guarantee?

    Ask what “implemented” means in the refund clause. Usually it means installed, not in use.

  3. 03

    Which entity and zone does the system think it is?

    Free-zone and mainland entities, designated zones and re-export all change the tax treatment. If the demo has one company, ask for two.

  4. 04

    What does a change request cost after go-live?

    The licence is rarely what hurts. Ask for the rate card for the pricing rule you know they will have to build.

  5. 05

    Who, by name, does the work — and where is my data?

    Two-hundred-person vendors rank here. Ask which three people are yours, where the database lives, and whether you can take it with you.

  6. 06

    Will you tell me to keep Tally and a spreadsheet?

    For a stable twelve-person trading firm, that is the right answer. Anyone who never gives it is selling.
