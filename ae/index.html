Reel 4The compliance layer

Nine things a UAE system has to get right, in one strip.

The ERP pages ranking here list these as features. We treat them as the floor. Figures and dates are as published by the FTA and the Ministry of Finance in September 2026; the system reads them from configuration so they can change without a developer.

VAT

VAT at 5%

FTA-compliant tax invoices, input/output reconciliation, VAT201 filing data from the ledger, designated-zone and reverse-charge handling. Built into the flow, not bolted on.

CT

Corporate Tax at 9%

Taxable income above the threshold, the small-business relief election, free-zone qualifying income, transfer-pricing documentation where it applies. The ERP keeps the books that make the return possible.

E-INV

E-invoicing, PINT-AE

The UAE’s Peppol-based PINT-AE format with accredited service providers; Phase 2 mandates from July 2026 per the current Ministry of Finance guidance. Invoices must leave the system already compliant.

WPS

WPS payroll

Salaries paid through the Wages Protection System with SIF files generated from payroll, MoHRE portal timing, and the fines that follow a late file avoided by design.

EOSB

Gratuity (EOSB)

End-of-service benefit accrued monthly under UAE Labour Law, per contract type and tenure, so the liability is a number in the accounts rather than a surprise at resignation.

ZONE

Free zone or mainland

JAFZA, DMCC, DIFC, KIZAD, Sharjah free zones: different licensing, VAT designated-zone status, customs and reporting. The system knows which entity it is invoicing from.

AR

Arabic where it counts

Bilingual invoices and RTL interfaces for the people who use them; English-first search pages, because 99% of the searches in the data are in English.

Ask any vendor, product or custom, to show one invoice go from despatch to PINT-AE submission to the ledger in a demo, with your data. Products that need a “localisation partner” for this are not UAE-ready, whatever the list says.
