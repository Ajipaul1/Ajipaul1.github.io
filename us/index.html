Why should a US company’s ERP know about sales-tax nexus?

Because since South Dakota v. Wayfair (21 June 2018), a state can require you to collect its sales tax without you ever having an office there. The Court overruled the old physical-presence rule and found South Dakota’s test — more than $100,000 of sales, or 200 or more separate transactions, into the state in a year — enough to create that duty. Every state with a sales tax now has an economic-nexus rule, and the lines still move: Illinois dropped its 200-transaction count on 1 January 2026. A spreadsheet will not warn you that a state you ship to twice a week just crossed its line. The systems we build total sales by ship-to state, flag a state as it nears its threshold, and hand your CPA an export instead of a scramble.

US Rules, Built In

Which US tax, accessibility and privacy rules does the work account for?

Eight rules a US build has to respect. We are engineers, not your lawyer or CPA: we build the records, notices and checks so the people who advise you have what they need. Every card links to its source.

🧾

Sales-tax economic nexus

Forty-five states levy a state-level sales tax and 38 allow local sales taxes, as of 1 January 2026. Remote-seller thresholds are set state by state; the most common is $100,000 of sales a year.

Source: Tax Foundation · Sales Tax Institute

📄

1099-NEC for contractors

For tax years after 2025 the Form 1099-NEC reporting threshold is $2,000, and anyone filing 10 or more information returns must e-file. The ERP keeps W-9 details and a running total per contractor.

Source: IRS, 1099-MISC/NEC instructions

💳

1099-K from payment platforms

Payment platforms and marketplaces report your receipts to the IRS once they total over $20,000 in more than 200 transactions, so your books have to reconcile to what Stripe, PayPal or Amazon files.

Source: IRS, understanding Form 1099-K

♿

ADA and web accessibility

The Justice Department’s March 2022 guidance says the ADA applies to what businesses open to the public offer on the web, and names WCAG as helpful technical guidance. We build and test to WCAG 2.1 AA.

Source: ADA.gov web guidance

🏛️

Selling to the public sector

State and local governments must meet WCAG 2.1 AA for web content and apps. After an April 2026 extension the dates are 26 April 2027 for entities of 50,000 people or more and 26 April 2028 for smaller ones.

Source: ADA.gov Title II web rule

🔐

California CCPA / CPRA

Reaches businesses above $26,625,000 in annual revenue, among other tests. Sellers or sharers of data need a “Do Not Sell or Share” link and must honour the Global Privacy Control signal; new risk-assessment rules started on 1 January 2026.

Source: CPPA thresholds · California AG · CPPA regulations

🗺️

The other state privacy laws

Twenty states now have comprehensive privacy laws on the books; Indiana, Kentucky and Rhode Island’s took effect on 1 January 2026. Consent, opt-outs and data requests are built as features, not a banner.

Source: MultiState, Feb 2026

📦

Inventory costing: FIFO or LIFO

US tax rules allow first-in first-out or last-in first-out stock valuation, and adopting LIFO takes IRS Form 970. The ERP keeps a cost layer for every receipt so your accountant can use either method.

Source: IRS Publication 538
